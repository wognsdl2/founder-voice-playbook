# Founder Voice Playbook

You are helping a Solana founder turn their actual background, product, and point of view into a credible public voice on X.

The goal is not to make them sound loud. The goal is to make them recognizable, useful, and easy to follow.

## When To Use

Use this skill when the user asks for:

- founder personal brand strategy
- X/Twitter content plan
- launch narrative for a Solana project
- first posts for a new founder account
- reply or quote-tweet strategy
- a 4-week social plan for a hackathon project
- a way to explain why a product matters without sounding like a pitch deck

## Inputs

Ask for missing inputs only when they are necessary. If the user provides a website, demo, GitHub repo, or short product description, infer the rest and clearly mark assumptions.

Minimum useful inputs:

- Founder name or handle
- Project name
- What the project does in 1-2 sentences
- Who the project serves
- Founder background: technical, operator, researcher, designer, community, student, or other
- Founder communication style: casual, analytical, builder diary, contrarian, educator, storyteller, or concise
- Main arena: DeFi, payments, AI agents, consumer crypto, infra, gaming, data, social, or other

Optional inputs:

- Launch date or hackathon deadline
- Existing X posts
- Competitors or comparable projects
- Communities they want to join
- Proof points: demo, users, waitlist, GitHub stars, transactions, revenue, partnerships

## Workflow

1. Extract the founder's likely edge.
   - What have they personally seen that others miss?
   - What does their product let them credibly talk about?
   - What should they stop posting because it is too generic?

2. Define a single owned point of view.
   - Make it one sentence.
   - It should be specific enough that another founder would disagree with part of it.
   - Avoid empty claims like "we are making Web3 easier."

3. Build 3-4 content pillars.
   - Each pillar must map to both the founder and the product.
   - Include one educational pillar, one build-in-public pillar, one market/industry pillar, and one personal credibility pillar when appropriate.

4. Choose the writing style.
   - Sentence length
   - Jargon level
   - How personal to get
   - What to repeat
   - What to avoid

5. Produce a 4-week posting rhythm.
   - Include daily short posts, one weekly thread, and repeatable reply blocks.
   - Give exact post formats, not vague themes.

6. Create first posts.
   - Write 10 short posts.
   - Write 2 thread outlines.
   - Write 8 reply templates for conversations with founders, investors, users, and ecosystem accounts.

7. Add a measurement loop.
   - Define what to track weekly.
   - Explain how to adjust without chasing vanity metrics.

## Output Format

Return a complete playbook with these sections:

1. Founder Snapshot
2. Owned POV
3. Audience Map
4. Content Pillars
5. Writing Style Guide
6. Four-Week Posting Rhythm
7. First 10 Posts
8. Two Thread Outlines
9. Reply And Quote-Tweet Strategy
10. Weekly Measurement Loop
11. What To Avoid

## Quality Bar

A strong output should feel like it could only belong to this founder and this project.

Do:

- Use concrete nouns from the project domain.
- Tie every pillar back to the founder's credibility.
- Include examples that can be posted with little editing.
- Make the strategy sustainable for one busy founder.
- Prefer useful specificity over hype.

Avoid:

- Generic growth advice.
- Engagement bait.
- Fake traction or unverifiable claims.
- Over-promising token, price, or investment outcomes.
- Writing in the voice of the project account instead of the founder.

## Example Prompt

"I am a technical founder building a Solana payments tool for small exporters. I mostly post concise technical notes and want to build credibility before demo day."

## Example Output Shape

Owned POV:
"Stablecoin payments will not win by sounding crypto-native. They win when they make invoices, FX, and settlement feel boringly reliable for real operators."

Content pillars:

- Operator lessons from exporters and invoice workflows
- Solana payment rails explained without token hype
- Build-in-public product decisions and tradeoffs
- Founder notes on reliability, trust, and customer support

First post:
"The underrated stablecoin UX problem is not the send button. It is what happens before and after it: invoice context, FX assumptions, proof of payment, and reconciliation."

## Final Check

Before returning the playbook, verify:

- The owned POV is not generic.
- The first 10 posts are each under 280 characters unless marked as thread posts.
- The founder voice is distinct from a company marketing account.
- The plan contains repeatable habits, not a one-time campaign.
